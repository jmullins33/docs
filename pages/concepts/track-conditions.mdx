# Track Conditions

Unlike pavement racing, dirt track surfaces change materially over the course of a race night — often enough to invert who the favorites are. A driver favored on a heavy, multi-groove surface can become a long shot by the feature if the track slicks off to a single groove.

---

## The condition score

Surface state is expressed as a single **condition score from 0 to 100**:

| Score | Surface | Betting implications |
|---|---|---|
| 0 – 20 | Dry / Slick | Single-groove; long-shot value for patient, technically sound drivers |
| 21 – 40 | Transitional slick | Second groove forming; setup versatility matters |
| 41 – 60 | Tacky / Ideal | Multiple grooves; most historical data here; lines most reliable |
| 61 – 80 | Heavy / Moisture-rich | Cushion forms; power and aggression rewarded; upsets more likely |
| 81 – 100 | Wet / Pre-race only | Feature rarely contested; high cancellation risk |

---

## Score components

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Moisture index (40% weight)">
    Derived from weather station readings, soil probe sensors (where available), and historical moisture retention curves for that specific track's soil composition. High moisture = high score.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Temperature differential (20% weight)">
    The delta between track surface temperature and ambient air temperature. A cold surface retains moisture longer; a hot surface accelerates drying. Measured via satellite thermal data or on-site sensors.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Wind and humidity (20% weight)">
    High wind accelerates evaporation and drying. Humidity slows it. The model uses a modified Penman evaporation equation tuned for packed clay.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Time-in-program decay (20% weight)">
    Even with no weather changes, track conditions dry down as the event progresses — more rubber is laid, the surface gets worked. The decay curve is calibrated per track from historical prep logs and lap count data.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

---

## How conditions move odds

A meaningful swing in the score (±10 points or more) triggers a full recalculation and an `odds.updated` webhook push.

Example: a pre-race condition score of 72 shifts to 55 after an unexpected dry wind picks up during hot laps. Drivers with strong heavy-track profiles see their implied probabilities drop; drivers who perform well at mid-range conditions see theirs rise.

The magnitude of the adjustment is driver-specific — it depends on each driver's **surface sensitivity score**, derived from their historical results split by condition band.

## Manual overrides

If your crew chief has a strong read on the surface, pass `track_condition_override` in your odds request:

```json
{
  "event_id": "evt_2026_eldora_dream",
  "market": "feature_winner",
  "track_condition_override": 71.0
}
```

Overrides replace the model's computed score for that request only. They do not affect future auto-calculated scores.

## Data sources (priority order)

| Source | Accuracy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| On-site weather station | Highest | 5-minute push; highest confidence scores |
| Nearest ASOS/AWOS station | Medium | Within 30 miles; accuracy decreases with distance |
| NWS gridded forecast | Lowest | 2.5km resolution; used for events >24h out |

## Per-track condition profiles

```bash
GET /tracks/{track_id}/condition-profile?series=lucas_oil_late_models
```

```json
{
  "track_id": "trk_eldora",
  "events_sampled": 14,
  "condition_profile": {
    "median_feature_score": 44.2,
    "most_common_band": "surface_tacky",
    "heavy_pct": 0.21,
    "tacky_pct": 0.57,
    "slick_pct": 0.22
  }
}
```

<Tip>
  Tracks with fewer than 3 sampled events use the regional track-type median as the condition prior.
</Tip>

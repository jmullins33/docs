## Why track conditions matter

Dirt tracks are not static surfaces. A track that starts heavy and tacky at hot laps can transition to a slick, one-groove speedway by the feature event — completely inverting who the favorites are. A driver who dominates heavy, cushion-heavy conditions may struggle when the bottom seals up, and vice versa.

Dirt IQ tracks this evolution continuously and incorporates it into every prediction update.

---

## The track condition score

Dirt IQ distills surface state into a single **track condition score** ranging from **0 to 100**:

| Score Range | Surface Description | Typical Racing Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| 0 – 20 | Dry / Slick | Single-groove racing; momentum and smooth driving rewarded |
| 21 – 40 | Transitional slick | Second groove beginning to form; power and braking points matter more |
| 41 – 60 | Tacky / Ideal | Multiple grooves racing; passing opportunities throughout the field |
| 61 – 80 | Heavy / Moisture-rich | Cushion forms on the outside; aggressive, horsepower-dependent driving rewarded |
| 81 – 100 | Wet / Extreme moisture | Usually pre-race only; feature rarely run in this range |

The score is a composite of four sub-components:

### Sub-components

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="droplet" title="Moisture index (40% weight)">
    Derived from weather station readings, soil probe sensors (where available), and historical moisture retention curves for that specific track's soil composition. High moisture = high score.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="thermometer" title="Temperature differential (20% weight)">
    The delta between track surface temperature and ambient air temperature. A cold surface retains moisture longer; a hot surface accelerates drying. Measured via satellite thermal data or on-site sensors.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="wind" title="Wind and humidity (20% weight)">
    High wind accelerates evaporation and drying. Humidity slows it. The model uses a modified Penman evaporation equation tuned for packed clay.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="clock" title="Time-in-program decay (20% weight)">
    Even with no weather changes, track conditions dry down as the event progresses — more rubber is laid, the surface gets worked. The decay curve is calibrated per track based on historical prep logs and lap count data.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Manual overrides

If your track has on-site sensors or your crew chief has a strong read on the surface, you can override the computed score when calling `/predictions`:

```json
{
  "event_id": "evt_2024_eldora_4crown",
  "division": "sprint_wing",
  "track_condition_override": 71.0
}
```

Overrides replace the model's computed score entirely for that prediction request. They do not affect future auto-calculated scores.

## Groove width and position

In addition to the composite score, Dirt IQ models **groove position** for select tracks with historical groove-mapping data:

- **Bottom groove** — tight, low line; favors smooth, mechanical drivers
- **Middle groove** — transitional line most common during tacky conditions
- **Top / cushion groove** — outside edge; favors horsepower and tire scrub during heavy conditions

Groove width data feeds into the driver metrics model to weight each driver's groove preference against current conditions.

## Track condition history

You can retrieve a track's historical condition scores via the API:

```bash
GET /tracks/{track_id}/conditions?date=2024-08-10
```

This returns an hour-by-hour condition log for the specified date, useful for:

- Post-race analysis
- Training custom models on top of Dirt IQ data
- Comparing predicted vs. actual condition evolution

## Adding your own track

Tracks not yet in the Dirt IQ database can be added by submitting:

1. Track name, location, and surface type
2. Configuration (oval, D-shaped, flat, banked — radius and banking angle)
3. Soil composition (clay type)
4. Historical results data (CSV export from your timing system)

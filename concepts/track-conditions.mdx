## Why track conditions move lines

Unlike pavement racing, dirt track surfaces change materially over the course of a race night — often enough to invert who the favorites are. A driver favored on a heavy, multi-groove surface can become a long shot by the feature if the track slicks off to a single groove. This surface volatility is one reason niche motorsport markets are difficult for generalist odds compilers to model correctly.

Dirt IQ's condition model directly addresses this by incorporating live surface state into every line recalculation.

---

## The condition score

Surface state is expressed as a single **condition score from 0 to 100**:

| Score | Surface | Betting implications |
|---|---|---|
| 0 – 20 | Dry / Slick | Single-groove racing; long-shot value for patient, technically sound drivers |
| 21 – 40 | Transitional slick | Starting to develop a second groove; setup versatility matters |
| 41 – 60 | Tacky / Ideal | Multiple grooves; most historical data collected here; lines most reliable |
| 61 – 80 | Heavy / Moisture-rich | Cushion forms on top; power and aggression rewarded; upsets more likely |
| 81 – 100 | Wet / Pre-race only | Feature rarely contested; high cancellation risk |

---

## Score components

| Component | Weight | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Moisture index | 40% | Weather station readings, soil probe sensors |
| Temperature differential | 20% | Satellite thermal data or on-site sensors |
| Wind / humidity | 20% | NWS or on-site station |
| Time-in-program decay | 20% | Track-specific empirical decay curves from DIRTRACKR lap count data |

The decay curve is the most important operator-facing variable — it describes how quickly a track dries under racing conditions independent of weather. It is calibrated per track using 3+ years of historical data where available.

---

## How conditions move odds

The ML regression layer uses the condition score as a direct input. A meaningful swing in the score (±10 points or more) triggers a full recalculation and a `odds.updated` webhook push.

Example: a pre-race condition score of 72 shifts to 55 after an unexpected dry wind picks up during hot laps. Drivers with strong heavy-track profiles (high win rates at score 61–80) see their implied probabilities drop; drivers who perform well at mid-range conditions see theirs rise.

The magnitude of the adjustment is driver-specific — it depends on each driver's **surface sensitivity score**, derived from their historical results split by condition band.

---

## Data sources

Dirt IQ pulls condition data in priority order:

1. **On-site weather station** — registered tracks with a 5-minute data push. Highest accuracy; highest confidence scores.
2. **Nearest ASOS/AWOS station** — airport weather within 30 miles. Used when no on-site station is registered.
3. **NWS gridded forecast** — 2.5km resolution. Used for pre-event projections and events >24h out.

## Track registration

To connect a track's on-site weather station, the track operator submits sensor readings to:

```
POST https://api.dirtiq.com/v1/tracks/{track_id}/conditions
```

```json
{
  "timestamp": "2024-08-16T19:35:00Z",
  "temp_f": 84.2,
  "humidity_pct": 52.0,
  "wind_mph": 8.3,
  "wind_direction_deg": 225,
  "precip_last_hour_in": 0.0,
  "surface_temp_f": 101.4
}
```

Connected tracks produce higher-confidence odds and are prioritized in series expansion.

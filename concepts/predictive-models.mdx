## Overview

Dirt IQ uses an ensemble of purpose-built models trained on over a decade of dirt track racing data. Each model targets a different prediction task — win probability, lap time projection, track condition evolution — and their outputs are combined via a meta-model to produce the final ranked predictions you see in the API response.

---

## Model architecture

### 1. Driver performance model

Predicts individual driver output based on recent form and career history. Inputs include:

- **Rolling performance score** — weighted average of finish positions over the last 30 days, decayed by recency
- **Surface-specific history** — separate statistics for clay, slick, and cushion conditions
- **Division-level calibration** — accounts for differences in field depth across series
- **Fatigue index** — penalizes drivers who have run high mileage in the previous 7 days

### 2. Track affinity model

Measures how well a driver historically performs at a specific track relative to the rest of the field:

- Baseline win rate and average finish at the track
- Condition-specific performance (driver may excel on slick but struggle on cushion at a given track)
- Qualifying and hot lap performance at the same venue

### 3. Track condition model

Forecasts how track conditions will evolve throughout the race night. See [Track Conditions](/concepts/track-conditions) for the full scoring methodology.

### 4. Weather impact model

Adjusts predictions based on real-time and forecast weather data. See [Weather Integration](/analytics/weather-integration) for details.

### 5. Meta-model (ensemble)

The four models above feed into a gradient-boosted meta-model that:

- Weights each model's signal based on how informative it is for the specific track and division
- Produces the final `win_probability`, `podium_probability`, and `top5_probability` values
- Outputs a `confidence` score representing the certainty of the overall prediction

## How predictions update during a race night

Predictions are not static. Dirt IQ recalculates throughout the event lifecycle:

```
Entry list published
        ↓
Initial prediction generated (driver + track affinity + weather)
        ↓
Hot laps / time trials complete
        ↓
Prediction updated with qualifying data + observed track conditions
        ↓
Heat races complete
        ↓
Prediction updated with heat race results (starting position, observed speed)
        ↓
Feature event start
        ↓
Real-time lap-by-lap updates (if live timing feed is connected)
```

Each recalculation fires a `prediction.updated` webhook event if you have a subscription registered.

## Confidence scores

The `confidence` field (0–1) reflects how much signal the models have for a given event. Confidence is higher when:

- The entry list is complete and all drivers have sufficient history
- Weather data is available from a local station
- Track prep logs have been submitted
- Heat race results are available

Confidence is lower when:

- The entry list contains many drivers with limited history at this track
- Weather data must be interpolated from distant stations
- The event uses a format the model hasn't seen many examples of

<Tip>
  When `confidence` is below 0.6, consider supplementing Dirt IQ predictions with domain expertise before making high-stakes decisions.
</Tip>

## Model limitations

| Limitation | Notes |
|---|---|
| First-time or rare drivers | Predictions for drivers with fewer than 5 career starts in the division have wider error bars |
| Unusual formats | Invert races, random draws, or non-standard feature lengths reduce model accuracy |
| Late DNQs / scratches | The model doesn't know about last-minute scratches until the entry list is updated |
| New tracks | Tracks added within the last 12 months have less historical data and lower confidence scores |

## Feedback and corrections

If you observe a systematic prediction error — for example, the model consistently undervalues a driver on slick tracks — you can submit feedback via the dashboard. Feedback is reviewed quarterly and used to retrain models.

Props are the side outcomes beyond who wins. Most reuse the same entry ratings,
just pointed at a different question.

## Supported props

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Fast time" icon="stopwatch">
    Who sets quick time in qualifying — driven mostly by recent qualifying pace.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Top five finish" icon="award">
    Probability a given driver finishes in the top five.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Hard charger" icon="arrow-up-right-dots">
    Most positions gained from start to finish.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Head-to-head" icon="code-compare">
    Which of two drivers finishes ahead of the other.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## How each is computed

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="stopwatch" title="Fast time">
    Rank entries by predicted qualifying pace and apply a softmax over that single
    feature. The fastest recent qualifiers rise to the top.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="award" title="Top five finish">
    Sum the probability mass of finishing 1st through 5th for a driver. A simple
    estimate: run many simulated finishing orders and count how often the driver
    lands in the top five.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="arrow-up-right-dots" title="Hard charger">
    Combine a strong driver rating with a deep starting spot. Positions gained is
    roughly `start_pos - expected_finish`, so the best hard-charger candidates are
    fast cars that start back in the field.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="code-compare" title="Head-to-head">
    Compare two ratings directly: `P(A beats B) = ratingA / (ratingA + ratingB)`.
    This is independent of the rest of the field.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Simulation approach

For props that depend on the full finishing order (top five, hard charger), a
Monte Carlo simulation is the most flexible option:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sample a finishing order">
    Draw a plausible finish for the field using the entry ratings as weights.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the outcomes">
    For that sampled race, note who hit top five, who gained the most spots, etc.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat and average">
    Run thousands of simulations. Each prop probability is the fraction of
    simulations in which it occurred.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Simulation lets you answer almost any prop ("does driver X finish ahead of Y and
  inside the top 10?") without writing a new formula for each one.
</Tip>

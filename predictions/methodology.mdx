Predictions start with a **driver rating** for a specific race. The rating blends
recent form, track fit, and qualifying pace into a single score that can be turned
into probabilities.

## Core features

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Recent form" icon="chart-line">
    Average finish and win rate over a driver's last N races, weighted toward the
    most recent.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Track fit" icon="location-dot">
    Historical performance at this track, and at tracks of similar length and
    configuration.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Qualifying pace" icon="stopwatch">
    Time-trial speed relative to the field — the strongest single-race signal.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Equipment & team" icon="wrench">
    Recent performance of the car/team, which captures mid-season changes.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## From features to a score

Each feature is normalized to a 0–1 scale, then combined with weights:

```text
rating = w1 * recent_form
       + w2 * track_fit
       + w3 * qualifying_pace
       + w4 * team_strength
```

<Note>
  Start with sensible weights (qualifying pace and recent form highest), then tune
  them by backtesting against past seasons. Don't over-fit — dirt racing is noisy.
</Note>

## Turning ratings into probabilities

To get win probabilities for a field, convert ratings with a softmax so they sum
to 100%:

$$P_i = \frac{e^{\,r_i / T}}{\sum_j e^{\,r_j / T}}$$

Here $$r_i$$ is the rating for driver $$i$$ and $$T$$ is a temperature that
controls how much the favorites are separated from the field. Lower $$T$$ makes
the model more confident in the top entries.

## Backtesting

<Steps>
  <Step title="Hold out a season">
    Train on prior seasons and predict a season the model has never seen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Score the predictions">
    Use log loss or Brier score for win probability, and hit rate for props.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tune and repeat">
    Adjust weights and the temperature, then re-run until the held-out score
    stops improving.
  </Step>
</Steps>
